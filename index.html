      How T-Trap works

      Fake assets sit inside the network where real users have no reason to go. Anything that touches them is treated as hostile, analysed, scored, and shown to the administrator right away.

      1

      Decoys and honeypots

      Fake files with canary tokens, mixed in with real-looking company files on the shared drive, plus a fake SSH service. There is no login wall to notice — touching a decoy is itself the trigger.

      2

      Log collector

      Every interaction is stored with source IP, time, service, and payload.

      AI threat analyzer

      Turns each event into features, picks the attack type, and gives a confidence score.

      Risk scoring and alerts

      Combines type, confidence, and volume into Low, Medium, High, or Critical. Decoy access is always Critical.

      Admin dashboard

      Live feed, map, timeline, top attackers, and a detail panel that explains each decision.

      About the AI module

      Features. Ports probed, failed logins, time window, login rate, bytes sent out, commands run, and whether a decoy was touched.

      Model. A feature-weighted classifier with a softmax output. It gives a probability for Reconnaissance, Brute-force, and Data Exfiltration, and that probability is the confidence you see on every alert.

      Evaluation. The table is computed live in your browser on freshly generated synthetic events, including deliberately ambiguous ones such as scanners that also try logins. It shows how the model behaves on simulated data. It is not a claim about real-world accuracy.

      Next step. Train a Random Forest in Python on a labelled attack dataset, then swap it in behind the same feature interface and report accuracy, precision, recall, and the confusion matrix on real data.
